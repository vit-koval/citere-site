How chatbots handle the same false claims in different countries and languages. Every figure is a bot × persona cell for one market; markets are compared side by side, never averaged.

5 markets · 6 bots · current runs Sep 2026 · watchlist v0.4 · judge v1.2

1. Repeat Rate — market × bot

For one persona at a time. Rows are markets, columns are bots. Sorted by how many bots have a confirmed problem in that market. Greyed cells have fewer than 20 answers.

<10% 10–20% 20–35% >35% · pooled over 9 claims · Wilson 95% CI

2. Where the problem is confirmed

Per market: how many bots have a repeat rate whose confidence interval excludes zero at P2, how many critical incidents, how many source-to-answer lines. No single score — the columns stay separate.

3. What has been tested where

Claims tested per market and cluster, with the run date and grid version. Empty cells are untested. Comparisons on this page are only made between markets sharing a grid version.

4. Silence map — where bots refuse instead of answering

Share of answers that were refusals, empty, or evasions. A market that looks clean because bots don't answer is not clean. Default persona P1 — refusals on neutral questions are the most telling.

<5% 5–15% 15–30% >30%

5. How often bots searched

Share of answers containing any citation. Separates "clean because it never looked" from "looked and stayed clean". Below: among answers that did cite something, how many cited a listed source.

6. Language of what bots cite vs language of the market

When a bot answers in German, does it cite German, English or Russian sources? Hatched segments are listed sources. The number under each bar is cross-language leakage: listed sources cited in a language other than the market's.

market language English Russian other · hatched = listed source

7. One bot, all markets

Pick a bot. Its repeat rate in every market, per persona, with a pairwise significance check: two markets differ when their intervals do not overlap.

8. One claim, all markets

Pick a claim. Where this specific lie gets through and where it doesn't. These are single-claim cells at 3 repeats — direction only; significance lives in block 7.

9. Which listed sources are local, which are global

A source is local to a market if 70% or more of its citations come from there; global if cited in three or more markets with none dominating. Local sources show where a language-specific mirror exists and works.

10. Grain-of-truth gap by market

Repeat rate on claims built around a real event (splice A/B/C) versus pure fabrications (splice D), per bot per market. The same gap everywhere means it is a property of the bots; different gaps mean it is a property of the market.

fabrications (D) grain-of-truth claims (A/B/C)

11. Language vs country

When two markets share a language but not a country, or a country but not a language, which one moves the numbers. Pairs are detected automatically from the runs.

12. Countermeasures by market

What has been done on each market and with whom. Counts by type; no ratios.

13. Trend by market

Repeat rate at P2 across runs, one line per market, one bot at a time. Appears once a market has two comparable runs.
