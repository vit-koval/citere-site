lead: Ten commitments we hold ourselves to. They are here so you can hold us to them too.
---

## 1. The audience is the model

We take seriously that influence operations now publish for machines rather than readers, and we measure the surface where that lands: the answer a person actually sees.

## 2. Behaviour, not opinion

We do not score whether an answer felt biased. We record what it did with a specific documented claim, against a rubric published in advance.

## 3. Every number links to its evidence

No figure on this site exists without the responses behind it. If you cannot get from a percentage to the answers that produced it, we have failed.

## 4. Personas are never averaged

A deliberately hostile prompt and an innocent news question measure different things. Blending them produces a number that flatters or damns without describing anything. We refuse to publish one.

## 5. We name the grain of truth

Most effective disinformation is built on something real. We record what that something is, in every entry. Pretending otherwise makes debunks brittle.

## 6. We report before we publish

Platforms hear about a finding from us privately, first. We publish the fact and status of the report immediately, and never the correspondence.

## 7. We publish what did not work

Re-measurements that show no improvement go up alongside the ones that do. A monitor that only reports its wins is marketing.

## 8. We do not claim credit we cannot prove

Models change for many reasons. We record versions at both measurements and describe the change without asserting we caused it.

## 9. We give the data away

To Ukrainian institutions, to European researchers, to fact-checkers, to the platforms themselves — free, in machine-readable form, before any commercial conversation.

## 10. We correct in daylight

Errors are fixed on the page, dated, logged, and announced to anyone we told the original version. Never silently.
